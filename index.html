01 / CLIMATE

Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term changes in temperatures and weather patterns. Human activities, particularly burning fossil fuels, increase greenhouse gases that trap heat in the atmosphere.

Why it matters A changing climate can affect communities, agriculture, water supplies, and natural ecosystems.
What can we do? Save electricity, use public transportation when practical, and support cleaner energy.
Click to enlarge ↗

02 / WASTE

Plastic Pollution

Plastic pollution happens when plastic products accumulate in the environment. Improperly managed waste can reach rivers, coastlines, and oceans.

Why it matters Plastic waste can harm wildlife, contaminate habitats, and break into smaller pieces that persist in nature.
What can we do? Carry reusable bags and bottles, sort waste properly, and avoid unnecessary single-use products.
Click to enlarge ↗

03 / FORESTS

Deforestation

Deforestation is the removal of forests for activities such as agriculture, development, and resource extraction. Forests provide habitats and help regulate natural systems.

Why it matters Forest loss can reduce wildlife habitats, increase erosion, and affect the carbon cycle and water systems.
What can we do? Support responsible forest management, reduce waste, and participate in appropriate tree-planting projects.
Click to enlarge ↗

04 / WILDLIFE

Biodiversity

Biodiversity describes the variety of living organisms, including plants, animals, fungi, and microorganisms, and the ecosystems in which they live.

Why it matters Healthy biodiversity supports food systems, pollination, ecosystem stability, and natural resources.
What can we do? Protect habitats, avoid disturbing wildlife, and support responsible conservation efforts.
